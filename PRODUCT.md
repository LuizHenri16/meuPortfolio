# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Recruiters & hiring managers** are the primary audience. They visit to evaluate technical fitness, craftsmanship, and cultural fit for development roles. They need to quickly assess skill depth, project quality, and professional maturity. They are time-constrained, often comparing multiple candidates, and make snap judgments about credibility within seconds.

Secondary audiences include potential freelance clients and fellow developers exploring for collaboration or inspiration.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that converts visitors into interview invitations and professional opportunities. Success means a recruiter finishes browsing with a clear, positive impression of Luiz's capabilities and reach out. The portfolio must demonstrate technical competence, design taste, and personal voice — it is simultaneously a credential and a piece of work in itself.

## Brand Personality

Creative, playful, distinctive. The portfolio should feel handcrafted and intentional — not templated or AI-generated. It walks a line between professional credibility and genuine personality; the work is serious but the presentation has soul. Warmth and curiosity should come through without sacrificing polish.

## Anti-references

- The generic AI-generated portfolio look: glassmorphism-by-default, samey card grids, tiny uppercase tracked labels above every section, generic gradient backgrounds, soft beige/sand palettes, stock-profile-photo vibes.
- Overly corporate or clinical design that suppresses personality.
- Template-like sites where every portfolio feels interchangeable.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach** — as a developer's portfolio, the craft of the site itself is a signal. Every interaction, animation, and layout choice reflects on the developer's ability.
2. **Show, don't tell** — let project work, visual evidence, and interaction speak louder than descriptive copy. The portfolio is a demonstration, not a résumé.
3. **Confident distinctiveness** — avoid safe defaults and generic patterns. Make deliberate choices that reflect a point of view, even if they risk not pleasing everyone.
4. **Respect the visitor's time** — recruiters scan fast. Hierarchy, clarity, and immediate signal are more important than decorative flourishes. Every element earns its place.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline target. Adequate color contrast (4.5:1 body text), keyboard-navigable, prefers-reduced-motion respected, meaningful alt text on images, semantic HTML structure.
